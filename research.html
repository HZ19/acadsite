Peer-reviewed publications

  • A meta-analysis of attitudes towards migrants and displaced persons

    S. Weber, N. Stoop, P. van der Windt and H. Zhai · British Journal of Political Science 55:e168, 2025 · doi.org/10.1017/S0007123425101075

    A systematic meta-analysis of 118 survey experiments (428,881 respondents across 53 countries) on what shapes attitudes toward migrants and displaced persons. Sociotropic economic concerns matter most: hosts are more welcoming toward migrants seen as net economic contributors. Cultural and humanitarian concerns shape reactions to specific migrant profiles, including a persistent anti-Muslim bias, with little evidence for simple labor-competition concerns.

  • A global ranking of research productivity of political science departments

    J. Barceló, C. Paik, P. van der Windt and H. Zhai · PS: Political Science & Politics 58(3), 574–585, 2025 · doi.org/10.1017/S1049096524001239

    Builds a global ranking of political science department research productivity from 115,427 articles and 12,696 books by 5,586 faculty across 178 departments worldwide, ranking programs by citations, journal impact, and top-publication counts for both overall and recent-period output.

  • New estimates of US Civil War mortality from full-census records

    J. Barceló, J. L. Jensen, L. Peisakhin and H. Zhai · Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 121(48), e2414919121, 2024 · doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2414919121

    Uses newly released full-count US census records and a migration-adjusted comparison method to produce new national and state-level estimates of Civil War mortality. The national estimate, roughly 698,000 deaths, is higher than the conventional historical figure, with mortality substantially greater in Confederate than Union states.

  • When parties move to the middle: the role of uncertainty

    J. Lindvall, D. Rueda and H. Zhai · British Journal of Political Science 53(4), 1208–1229, 2023 · doi.org/10.1017/S0007123422000758

    Argues that whether main parties converge toward the median voter or diverge toward their base depends on how much uncertainty they face about where that voter actually stands. Tracking party platforms across twenty democracies since the 1960s, parties move toward the center after informative elections and apart after uncertain ones.

  • If not now, when? Climate disaster and the green vote following the 2021 Germany floods

    S. Garside and H. Zhai · Research & Politics 9(4), 137401, 2022 · doi.org/10.1177/20531680221141523

    A difference-in-differences study of the 2021 Germany floods, which struck two months before a federal election, testing whether firsthand experience of a climate disaster shifted voter support toward the Green Party.

  • Presidential elections and cryptocurrency returns: evidence from 2020 and 2024

    H. Zhai · read the paper (PDF)

    An event-study comparison of cryptocurrency returns around the 2020 and 2024 US presidential elections. Both show similar pre-election declines, but post-election paths diverge sharply: returns stayed depressed for a month in 2020, while surging over +30% by the same point in 2024, a pattern linked to differing market expectations about incoming crypto regulation.
